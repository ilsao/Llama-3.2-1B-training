\begin{abstract}
We document an iterative study of randomly initialized Llama 3.2 1B students trained on Dolma with a fixed 8192-token context, tracing how optimizer, schedule, initialization, data-selection, attention-mask, and supervision experiments changed subsequent decisions.
The central challenge is comparing recipes fairly when batch size changes the update clock, evaluation subsets overlap, shared-checkpoint branches inherit training, and startup failures consume resources.
The study progresses from AdamW through a tuned intradoc NorMuon recipe to full-vocabulary online distillation from a frozen pretrained 3B teacher, retaining negative results and checkpoint-aligned provenance.
The best verified six-billion-input recipe achieves combined-10k perplexity 12.3989 versus 12.9078 for its preceding non-distilled recipe, a \KDImprovement\% reduction at 64.711 H200-hours; a later adaptive-batch plus early-decay candidate uses \CtwoUpdateReduction\% fewer updates but yields worse perplexity 12.5380 at 65.009 H200-hours.
The report preserves 20 formal identities and all 327 recorded allocations, totaling \CombinedHours{} local H200-hours including separately recorded preprocessing, and distinguishes these observed results from unmeasured full-50k performance, statistical significance, and universal method claims.
\end{abstract}
